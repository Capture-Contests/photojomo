An Island Defined by Its Heritage.

Storied, Sovereign, and Quietly Spectacular.

Barbados sits at the easternmost edge of the Caribbean. Its landscape is not one of volcanic drama, it is coral limestone, patient and ancient, carved into low hills, the chattel house, the flying fish, the Garrison, the rum — each carries a civilisation. Capture Barbados is an invitation to photograph the depth beneath the surface.

The Chattel House Initiative

In Partnership with the Barbados National Trust

The chattel house is the most honest architectural object in the Caribbean — built to be moved, designed to be owned by those who were not allowed to own the land beneath them. Capture Barbados is creating the first comprehensive photographic archive of Barbados’ remaining chattel houses, room by room, parish by parish, before time and development remove them from the landscape.

An Island That Built Its Own World

Barbados became a republic in 2021, completing a journey begun centuries earlier by those who refused to be reduced to their circumstances.

The island has given the Caribbean its first UNESCO-inscribed garrison, its most celebrated rum tradition, and a craft culture from mahogany furniture to tuk band music to flying fish preparation, that is entirely its own.
